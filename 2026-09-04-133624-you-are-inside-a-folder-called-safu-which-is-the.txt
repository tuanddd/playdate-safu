  Your question: yes, 50 is a hardware cap. The Playdate uses a Sharp
  memory-in-pixel LCD whose panel refresh tops out at 50Hz, so
  playdate.display.setRefreshRate() accepts nothing higher — asking for 60 just
  gets you 50. The default is 30; I set it to 50 because dial tracking is the
  whole game and the extra 20 frames noticeably tighten the crank-to-dial feel.
  Battery cost is real but irrelevant for 5-minute sessions.

  Changes:

  1. BGM 0.14 → 0.12.
  2. Tick spacing doubled again — sound now fires every 4 dial units instead of
     every 2.
  3. Dial shake on sweet spot — replaced the random jitter with a damped sine
     oscillation: 4.5px amplitude decaying to zero over 220ms, so it reads as a
     mechanical thunk rather than noise.
  4. Level-cleared jingle — converted to WAV, plays 1000ms after the handle
     turns. The old victory sound (heavy click + double bolt + hinge) is gone.
  5. A turns the handle. The 3rd sweet spot no longer ends the level — it looks
     and sounds identical to the 1st and 2nd (same K-CHIK!, same click), so you
     genuinely can't tell. Press A when you believe all three are in. Wrong
     count → LOCKED!, a dull clunk, and a full reset to tumbler 1.

  One judgment call to flag: I kept a short mechanical clunk at the moment you 
  press A — bolt + hinge, ~400ms — before the 1s gap and the jingle. Without it,
  pressing A gave a full second of silence, which read as a dropped input
  rather than a handle turning. If you meant strip that too so it's
  silence-then-jingle, say so and it's a one-line change.

  Also added a small static A - HANDLE label at the bottom of the play screen —

  information.
