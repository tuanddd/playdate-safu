❯ when designing the HUD, are you already aware of the Playdate's screen size

⏺ Yes — and here's exactly what I honoured versus where I'm knowingly outside
  the guideline.

  Honoured
  - 400x240, authored 1:1. No scaling, no viewBox tricks. Every coordinate in
    build-hud.js is a device pixel.
  - 1-bit, not greyscale. The renderer hard-thresholds at 128 after rasterising,
    so no anti-aliased grey survives. What you see is what the panel can show.
  - Dither on exact pixel grids. The d12/d25/d50/d75 patterns are 2px and 4px
    cells; at 1:1 they land on whole device pixels instead of shimmering.
  - @2x is nearest-neighbour off the finished 1-bit bitmap, not a re-render at
    double size. It magnifies real pixels rather than flattering them.
  - Retina-class density (173 ppi, ~2.7"). That's the donaldhays.com point in
    CLAUDE.md: art reads about half as large physically as it does on your
    monitor. It's why I kept the dial dominant and the furniture chunky.

  Where I'm outside it — two real risks

  1. The 8.5px subtitles are too small. The Playdate art guide says body text
     11–13px and calls 8px unreadable. DIAL HIDDEN / TICKS DUCKED will be mush
     on the device, even though they look fine at 2x. Same concern for the 10px
     dial numerals at r=52.
  2. The mockups use macOS fonts, not the game's. Helvetica and Menlo, not
     Nontendo-Bold / Roobert-11 / Asheville-Mono-24. Metrics will shift on port
     — card text will need re-fitting against the real bitmap fonts.

  One inherited item: the dial's minor tick marks are 1px, and the guide says
  nothing thinner than ~2px. That comes from Art.drawDial in the current build,
  not from these mockups.

  Next: I can re-render variant 1 with the subtitles at 11px and the real
  Roobert-11 metrics, so you see whether the cards still fit before I write any
  Lua. Want that?

⏺ HAIR TRIGGER = you must crank absurdly slowly to catch a tumbler. It's the
  Motor-axis modifier that drops MAX_ENGAGE_SPEED from 0.5 to 0.15.

  The rule it changes

  There's already a speed gate on latching. Crossing a sweet spot too fast gives
  you a graze instead of a hit — a dulled tick, TOO FAST on screen, and a reset
  of your progress if you were past tumbler 1. Normally that ceiling is
  generous:

  ┌─────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────┬──────────────────┐

  Why it's interesting

  It attacks the exact thing the core loop rewards. Normally you learn to sweep
  quickly and slow down when you sense you're near. HAIR TRIGGER punishes the
  sweep-then-brake habit, because braking after you feel the zone is already too
  late — you have to be slow before you arrive. It turns the whole run into a
  crawl and makes the 60-second clock bite.

  RESET_SPEED (1.6, the wild-spinning gate) is untouched — only the latching
  ceiling moves.

  painfully gentle. The thing that's hair-thin is your speed budget, not the
  trigger. FEATHER TOUCH, CRAWL, or DELICATE would each teach the mechanic on
